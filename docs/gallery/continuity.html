Continuity and capture: reproducing Bregman & Ahad

Every sound on this page is sonore's reproduction of a published demonstration. All but one are from Albert Bregman and Pierre Ahad's Demonstrations of Auditory Scene Analysis (1996), the compact disc that accompanies Bregman's book Auditory Scene Analysis (1990); one, the pulsation threshold, is from the Acoustical Society of America's Auditory Demonstrations (Houtsma, Rossing & Wagenaars, 1987). The demonstrations, their design and what they show are their authors' work. Bregman's laboratory publishes the original recordings and booklet (McGill), and they are the ones to listen to.

What they show. When a sound suddenly gets louder or richer, hearing tends to take the change as the old sound carrying on with a new sound added on top. Bregman calls this the old-plus-new heuristic. It has three consequences that the examples below make audible. A tone heard just before a mixture can capture its match out of the mixture, so the mixture loses that part. A soft sound cut off by a louder sound that could have hidden it is heard continuing behind the loud one. And what is left of the mixture once the old sound is taken out, the residual, is heard as a new sound of its own. Streams is about sounds grouped one after another and Fusion about sounds grouped at the same time; this page is about the two kinds of grouping competing. Each example opens with what to listen for, taken from what the booklet reports listeners hear.

This page is a test of sonore: can its generators rebuild each stimulus from the description in the booklet? Each example names the demonstration it reproduces, rebuilds it from the booklet's frequencies, levels and timing, and says where it departs from them: where the booklet leaves a value open, where a recording is replaced by a stand-in, or where a long track is shortened. What listeners hear is reported as the booklet reports it; this page has not tested any of it.

  • Capturing a tone out of a mixture: a tone pulled out of a two-tone chord by the tones before it (Bregman & Ahad, Demonstrations 25 to 27).
  • Continuing through noise: tones, glides and speech heard carrying on behind loud noise (Demonstrations 28 to 32; Houtsma et al., Demonstration 11).
  • What is left over: the residual heard as a sound of its own (Demonstrations 33 to 35).
  • Glides and a vowel: capturing a glide out of a mixture of glides, and changing a vowel by capturing one of its harmonics (Demonstrations 36 and 37).
Setup, the same on every page
import os
import urllib.request

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

import sonore as so

plt.rcParams.update({"font.size": 9, "axes.titlesize": 10, "figure.dpi": 100})


def fetch(path):
    """A file from the sonore repository, by its path there: the local copy when this runs from
    the repository root, otherwise downloaded from GitHub to the same relative path."""
    if not os.path.exists(path):
        os.makedirs(os.path.dirname(path) or ".", exist_ok=True)
        urllib.request.urlretrieve("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/choyun1/sonore/main/" + path, path)
    return path


def finish(snd):
    """How every sound in the gallery is played: 5 ms ramps, RMS 0.1, peak at most 0.95."""
    snd = snd.ramp(5e-3).normalize(rms=0.1)
    return snd.normalize(peak=0.95) if snd.peak > 0.95 else snd

Press play and a line follows the sound across every time axis in its plots. Click any time axis to play from that point. Start with your volume low.

Code the examples share

The tones are placed at their onset times, as on the Streams page; tones and noises that switch on and off over a longer sound are multiplied by on, a level that is 1 inside given stretches of time and 0 outside them. Like every sonore generator, so.pure_tone, so.harmonic_complex and so.gaussian_noise scale the sound they make to RMS 1, so each pure tone and each glide here has RMS 1 and the booklets' level relations are set by scaling from there. finish keeps those relations, scaling only the whole sound. The sample rate is 22.05 kHz, except for the speech, which keeps its recording's 16 kHz.

Code
FS = 22050


def envelope(n, rise, decay):
    """Linear rise and decay ramps, in seconds, over `n` samples."""
    i = np.arange(n)
    return np.clip(np.minimum(i / (rise * FS), (n - 1 - i) / (decay * FS)), 0, 1)


def tone(freq, dur, rise=10e-3, decay=10e-3):
    """A sine tone at RMS 1 (before its ramps), `dur` seconds long including linear ramps."""
    snd = so.pure_tone(dur, FS, freq, phase=-np.pi / 2)
    return so.Sound(envelope(snd.n_samples, rise, decay) * snd.data[:, 0], FS)


def place(events, dur=None):
    """Sounds added at their onset times: `events` is a list of (onset in seconds, Sound)."""
    end = max(onset + snd.duration for onset, snd in events)
    out = np.zeros((round((dur or end) * FS) + 1, 1))
    for onset, snd in events:
        i = round(onset * FS)
        out[i : i + snd.n_samples] += snd.data[: len(out) - i]
    return so.Sound(out, FS)


def on(t, starts, length, ramp=5e-3):
    """1 for `length` seconds from each of `starts` and 0 elsewhere, changing along straight ramps
    `ramp` seconds long centered on each edge (abruptly if `ramp` is 0). One minus it is 1 exactly
    where it is not, so a sound gated by `on` and another by `1 - on` cross-fade."""
    level = np.zeros_like(t)
    for start in starts:
        if ramp:
            level += np.clip(np.minimum(t - start, start + length - t) / ramp + 0.5, 0, 1)
        else:
            level += (t >= start) & (t < start + length)
    return level


def glide(f_start, dur, octaves_per_second, ramp=10e-3):
    """A pure glide at RMS 1 (before its ramps), straight on a log-frequency axis, from `f_start`
    for `dur` seconds, with linear ramps."""
    times = np.linspace(0, dur, 101)
    contour = (times, f_start * 2 ** (octaves_per_second * times))
    snd = so.harmonic_complex(dur, FS, contour, harmonics=[1], ramp=0)
    return so.Sound(envelope(snd.n_samples, ramp, ramp) * snd.data[:, 0], FS)


def show(snd, fmin=100, fmax=5000, title="Spectrogram (Hann 30 ms)", log=True):
    """A spectrogram, on a log frequency axis unless `log` is False. Returns the figure and the
    panels the playhead follows."""
    fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(10, 3.2), layout="constrained")
    stft = so.STFT(snd, win_dur=30e-3, hop_dur=5e-3)
    keep = (stft.f >= fmin) & (stft.f <= fmax)
    top = stft.db[:, keep].max()
    ax.pcolormesh(stft.t, stft.f[keep], stft.db[0][keep], vmin=top - 60, vmax=top, cmap="magma")
    if log:
        so.plotting.log_frequency_axis(ax)
    ax.set(ylim=(fmin, fmax), ylabel="Frequency [Hz]", title=title)
    ax.set(xlabel="Time [s]", xlim=(0, snd.duration))
    return fig, [ax]

Capturing a tone out of a mixture

Two tones that start and stop together tend to fuse into one sound. A tone heard just before them, close in frequency to one of the two, can pull that one away into a stream of its own. Which grouping wins depends on how strong each pull is.

A far above the chord

Listen for the high tone A and the upper tone B of the chord: can you keep hearing the alternating A B A B of the opening (the standard) once the low tone C joins B? Here A is at 1800 Hz, far above B, and the booklet expects B to fuse with C into one richer tone that alternates with a pure A. The next two sounds bring A closer to B.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 25, a pattern discussed in Bregman (1990). Pure tones of equal amplitude: B at 650 Hz and C at 300 Hz always, and A at 1800, 1200 or 650 Hz in the three examples. Tones and the silences between them are 200 ms long, the tones with 10 ms rise and decay, so the cycle is A, silence, B and C together, silence. Four cycles of the standard (A and B alone) come first, as in the booklet, then six of the full pattern, where the booklet plays twelve. The booklet reports that the closer A is to B, the more easily B is heard going with A rather than with C.

cc1_a_far_above_the_chord.flac, 8.0 s, mono

Code
CYCLE = 0.8


def capture(a, b=650, c=300, c_delay=0.0, n_standard=4, n_full=6):
    """The A, B-and-C cycle after its A-B standard; C starts and stops `c_delay` seconds after B."""
    events = []
    for k in range(n_standard + n_full):
        start = CYCLE * k
        events += [(start, tone(a, 0.2)), (start + 0.4, tone(b, 0.2))]
        if k >= n_standard:
            events.append((start + 0.4 + c_delay, tone(c, 0.2)))
    return place(events, dur=CYCLE * (n_standard + n_full))


sound = finish(capture(1800))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=200, fmax=2500)

A fourth tone that frees the chord

Listen for the standard, A B, inside each of the two patterns that follow it. In the first the chord is followed by a silence; in the second it is followed by a fourth tone D, just below C. The booklet reports that A and B are easier to hear together in the second pattern: D pulls C into a stream of its own, which loosens C's hold on B, so B is freer to go with A, although neither A nor B has changed.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 27, after Bregman & Tougas (1989). Pure tones of equal amplitude, 200 ms long with 10 ms rise and decay: A 1900, B 1200, C 650 and D 613 Hz, with no silences between A, the chord of B and C, and D (or the silence in its place), all as in the booklet. Ours: the number of cycles (four of the standard, then ten of each pattern) and the 1 s pause between the two.

cc3_a_fourth_tone_that_frees_the_chord.flac, 17.8 s, mono

Code
def with_or_without_d(d, start):
    events = []
    for k in range(14):
        t = start + 0.6 * k
        events += [(t, tone(1900, 0.2)), (t + 0.2, tone(1200, 0.2))]
        if k >= 4:
            events.append((t + 0.2, tone(650, 0.2)))
            if d:
                events.append((t + 0.4, tone(613, 0.2)))
    return events


sound = finish(place(with_or_without_d(False, 0.0) + with_or_without_d(True, 9.4)))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=400, fmax=2500)

Continuing through noise

A soft sound cut off by a burst of louder noise is heard carrying on behind the noise, as long as the noise could have hidden it. With a silence in the cut instead, the sound is heard stopping and starting.

A tone and a noise in its gap

Listen for a steady tone that breaks off for a tenth of a second every second. The gap holds silence at first, then a burst of noise that gets louder in steps. The booklet reports that at the loudest step the tone seems to stay on behind the noise without a break, and invites you to judge whether it does at any of the earlier steps.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 28, after Warren (1982). A 1000 Hz tone for 900 ms alternating with 100 ms of white noise, with the noise's amplitude stepping through 0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5 and 1 of its final level. At the final level the booklet gives the noise's overall amplitude as about 20 times the tone's, which is the RMS ratio here. Ours: two cycles at each level (the booklet does not give the number) and cross-fades of 5 ms between tone and noise.

cc4_a_tone_and_a_noise_in_its_gap.flac, 14.0 s, mono

Code
t = np.arange(round(14 * FS)) / FS
gaps = on(t, 0.9 + np.arange(14), 0.1)
steps = np.repeat([0, 0.025, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5, 1], 2)
noise_level = 20 * steps[np.minimum(t.astype(int), 13)]
noise = so.gaussian_noise(14, FS, rng=np.random.default_rng(28)).data[: len(t), 0]
tone_1000 = so.pure_tone(14, FS, 1000).data[: len(t), 0]
sound = finish(so.Sound((1 - gaps) * tone_1000 + gaps * noise_level * noise, FS))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=100, fmax=10000)

A tone stepping down between noise bursts

Listen for a tone that alternates with a narrow band of noise around its own frequency, and softens one step every four tones. At first the tone is heard pulsing on and off; the booklet reports that below some level, the pulsation threshold, it seems to sound on continuously through the noise.

Houtsma, Rossing & Wagenaars, Demonstration 11, after Houtgast (1972). A 2000 Hz tone for 125 ms alternating with 125 ms of noise from 1875 to 2125 Hz. The tone starts at the noise's level and drops 1 dB after every four tones, in 15 steps, while the noise stays the same, all as in the booklet. The cross-fades of 5 ms are ours; the booklet plays the series once and so does this reproduction.

cc5_a_tone_stepping_down_between_noise_bursts.flac, 16.0 s, mono

Code
t = np.arange(round(16 * FS)) / FS
noise_slots = on(t, 0.125 + 0.25 * np.arange(64), 0.125)
tone_db = -np.floor(t / 1.0)  # four tones a second, 1 dB down after every four
band = so.gaussian_noise(16, FS, band=(1875, 2125), rng=np.random.default_rng(11)).data[: len(t), 0]
tone_2000 = so.pure_tone(16, FS, 2000).data[: len(t), 0]
sound = finish(so.Sound((1 - noise_slots) * 10 ** (tone_db / 20) * tone_2000 + noise_slots * band, FS))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=1000, fmax=3000, log=False)

A glide cut in the middle of its rises and falls

Listen for a tone gliding up and down, with a short piece cut out of the middle of every rise and every fall. For the first three cycles the cuts are silent; then they hold loud noise, and the booklet reports the glide heard as complete, carrying on behind the noise.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 29, after Dannenbring (1976). A pure tone gliding between 1464 and 2297 Hz, straight on a log-frequency axis, a full cycle up and down taking 2 s. The cuts are 0.15 s long; the noise is white with a peak amplitude 21 times the tone's, all as in the booklet. Ours: three cycles of each, and cross-fades of 5 ms.

cc6_a_glide_cut_in_the_middle_of_its_rises_and_falls.flac, 13.0 s, mono

Code
LOW, HIGH = 1464, 2297
t = np.arange(round(6 * FS)) / FS
times = np.linspace(0, 6, 6001)
triangle = 1 - np.abs((times + 0.25) % 2 - 1)  # 0 at the bottom, 1 at the top; starts on the way up
glide_tone = so.harmonic_complex(6, FS, (times, LOW * (HIGH / LOW) ** triangle), harmonics=[1], ramp=0)
glide_tone = glide_tone.data[: len(t), 0]
rng = np.random.default_rng(29)


def cut_glide(centers, width, noise):
    """The glide with `width`-second cuts around `centers`, filled with noise if `noise`."""
    cuts = on(t, np.asarray(centers) - width / 2, width)
    filler = 0
    if noise:
        burst = so.gaussian_noise(6, FS, rng=rng).data[: len(t), 0]
        peak = max(np.abs(burst[cuts > 0.5 * (cuts.max())]).max(), 1e-9)
        filler = 21 * np.sqrt(2) * burst / peak  # the tone's peak is sqrt(2)
    return so.Sound((1 - cuts) * glide_tone + cuts * filler, FS).ramp(10e-3)


def silent_then_noise(centers, width):
    return so.concat([cut_glide(centers, width, False), so.silence(1.0, FS), cut_glide(centers, width, True)])


mid_legs = 0.25 + np.arange(6)  # halfway up and halfway down, in this sound's time
sound = finish(silent_then_noise(mid_legs, 0.15))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=500, fmax=10000)

The top cut out, silent

Listen for the highest pitch the glide reaches. The top of each cycle is cut out, first by a wide silent cut, then by a narrow one. The booklet points out that the glide reaches higher before the narrow cut, as the spectrogram shows. The next sound fills the same cuts with noise.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 30, after Dannenbring (1976). The glide of Demonstration 29, cut only at its top, by 400 ms in the first three cycles and by 100 ms in the next three, as in the booklet. Ours: three cycles of each, and a pause of 1 s between them.

cc7_the_top_cut_out_silent.flac, 13.0 s, mono

Code
tops = 0.75 + np.arange(0, 6, 2)


def top_cut(width, noise):
    cuts = on(t, tops - width / 2, width)
    return so.Sound((1 - cuts) * glide_tone + cuts * noise, FS).ramp(10e-3)


narrow_band = so.gaussian_noise(6, FS, band=(1500, 3000), rng=np.random.default_rng(30)).data[: len(t), 0]
sound = finish(so.concat([top_cut(0.4, 0), so.silence(1.0, FS), top_cut(0.1, 0)]))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=1000, fmax=4000)

The top cut out, with noise

Listen for the highest pitch the glide seems to reach behind the noise, wide cut first and narrow cut second. If hearing carried each rise and fall on to where they would have met, both would seem to reach the same top. The booklet reports instead that the top seems higher with the narrow cut, and concludes that hearing joins the highest ends it actually heard rather than extrapolating them.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 30, its second part: the same cuts filled with noise from 1500 to 3000 Hz, whose spectrum level the booklet gives as about 3 dB above the tone's. The booklet's Demonstration 28 says that white noise 20 times the tone's amplitude has the tone's spectrum level on its disc, which was sampled at 22255 Hz; taking that as the meaning of spectrum level, a 1500 Hz band 3 dB higher has an RMS about 10.4 times the tone's, the level used here. That reading is ours.

cc7b_the_top_cut_out_with_noise.flac, 13.0 s, mono

Code
noise_rms = np.sqrt(2 * 20**2 * 1500 / (22255 / 2))
filler = noise_rms * narrow_band
sound = finish(so.concat([top_cut(0.4, filler), so.silence(1.0, FS), top_cut(0.1, filler)]))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=1000, fmax=4000)

A sentence through a picket fence

Listen for the sentence. Every other sixth of a second is cut out, first leaving silent gaps, then filled with loud noise; last comes the sentence intact. The booklet reports that with the noise the speech is heard as continuing behind it, though not always clearly, which it calls the picket-fence effect, after seeing an object as whole behind the pickets of a fence.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 31, after Miller & Licklider (1950) and Warren (1982). A stand-in for its recording: the male talker's sentence used on the Fusion page (CMU ARCTIC, speaker bdl), where the disc has two sentences of its own. The speech is switched on and off abruptly by a 3 Hz square wave, so speech and gaps are each a sixth of a second; the noise is switched on and off abruptly to fill the gaps, falls 6 dB an octave, and has an RMS twice that of the loudest stretch of speech, all as in the booklet. The noise's band, 100 to 8000 Hz, is ours.

cc8_a_sentence_through_a_picket_fence.flac, 9.1 s, mono

Code
speech = so.load(fetch("docs/speech/bdl_arctic_a0131.flac"))
speech_t = speech.t
rng = np.random.default_rng(31)
tilted = so.gaussian_noise(speech.duration, speech.fs, band=(100, 8000), tilt=-6, rng=rng)


def picket_fence(rate):
    """The sentence with every other half-period of a `rate` Hz square wave cut out: silent
    gaps, then the same gaps filled with noise."""
    half = 0.5 / rate
    starts = np.arange(0, speech.duration, 2 * half)
    kept = on(speech_t, starts, half, ramp=0)
    segments = [speech.data[(speech_t >= start) & (speech_t < start + half), 0] for start in starts]
    loudest = max(np.sqrt(np.mean(segment**2)) for segment in segments)
    gapped = so.Sound(kept * speech.data[:, 0], speech.fs)
    filled = so.Sound(gapped.data[:, 0] + (1 - kept) * 2 * loudest * tilted.data[:, 0], speech.fs)
    return gapped, filled


pause = so.silence(0.75, speech.fs)
gapped, filled = picket_fence(3)
sound = finish(so.concat([gapped, pause, filled, pause, speech]))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=100, fmax=8000, log=False)

A noise swelling slowly, then suddenly

Listen for a hiss that swells and fades in each cycle at first. Then the swells become short and sudden, and the booklet reports a different sound: one long, steady hiss, briefly joined by a second, louder one. It calls this homophonic continuity: the soft noise is heard going on unchanged, and the extra energy of the sudden rise is heard as a new sound.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 32, after Warren, Obusek & Ackroff (1972). White noise whose amplitude rests at 0.25 of its peak. In the gradual cycle, 744 ms in all, it holds for 120 ms, rises in a straight line to 1 in 252 ms, falls back in 252 ms and holds for 120 ms. In the sudden cycle, 724 ms in all, it holds for 342 ms, rises in 1 ms, stays at 1 for 32 ms, falls in 1 ms and holds for 348 ms; all as in the booklet. Ours: five cycles of each, and a pause of 1 s between them.

Code
def swells(knots, levels, n_cycles=5):
    period = knots[-1]
    t = np.arange(round(n_cycles * period * FS)) / FS
    noise = so.gaussian_noise(t[-1] + 1 / FS, FS, rng=np.random.default_rng(32)).data[: len(t), 0]
    return so.Sound(np.interp(t % period, knots, levels) * noise, FS).ramp(10e-3)


gradual = swells([0, 0.120, 0.372, 0.624, 0.744], [0.25, 0.25, 1, 0.25, 0.25])
sudden = swells([0, 0.342, 0.343, 0.375, 0.376, 0.724], [0.25, 0.25, 1, 1, 0.25, 0.25])
sound = finish(so.concat([gradual, so.silence(1.0, FS), sudden]))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=100, fmax=10000)

What is left over

When the old sound is taken out of a mixture, what is left, the residual, is heard as a new sound of its own, even though nothing in the signal is shaped like it.

Two harmonics capturing their match

Listen for a rich low tone at first. Then it alternates with a thinner tone that holds only two of its harmonics, first with silences between them and then with none. The booklet reports that with the silences a second, higher tone is heard in the rich one only weakly, if at all, but without them it is heard clearly: the three harmonics the thinner tone does not have.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 33. B, the rich tone, holds harmonics 1, 3, 5, 6 and 9 of 300 Hz at 0, -20, -28, -33 and -38 dB; A, the captor, holds harmonics 1 and 3 at the same levels. A cycle lasts 1 s: with silences, A for 200 ms, a 200 ms silence, B for 400 ms and another 200 ms silence; without, A for 600 ms and then B for 400 ms. First B alone, then the two alternations, as in the booklet. Ours: six cycles of each where the booklet plays eight, 10 ms ramps, and a pause of 1 s between the three. Here A and B are one sound whose harmonics 5, 6 and 9 switch on and off, so where A and B meet, harmonics 1 and 3 carry on unbroken.

cc10_two_harmonics_capturing_their_match.flac, 20.0 s, mono

Code
LEVELS_DB = {1: 0, 3: -20, 5: -28, 6: -33, 9: -38}
N_CYCLES = 6
B_ONSETS = np.arange(N_CYCLES) + 0.4  # in each 1 s cycle, B from 0.4 to 0.8 s
PART = N_CYCLES + 1.0
starts = {"B alone": 0.0, "with silences": PART, "no silences": 2 * PART}


def harmonics_on(t, n):
    """Harmonic `n`'s level over all three parts."""
    b = sum(on(t, start + B_ONSETS, 0.4, ramp=10e-3) for start in starts.values())
    if n not in (1, 3):
        return b
    a_with_silences = on(t, starts["with silences"] + B_ONSETS - 0.4, 0.2, ramp=10e-3)
    a_without = on(t, starts["no silences"] + B_ONSETS - 0.6, 0.6, ramp=10e-3)
    # the first A of the last part starts with the part, not 0.2 s before it
    return b + a_with_silences + a_without * (t >= starts["no silences"])


def residual_levels(t, f, n):
    return 10 ** (LEVELS_DB[n] / 20) * np.clip(harmonics_on(t, n), 0, 1)


sound = so.harmonic_complex(
    3 * PART - 1.0, FS, 300, harmonics=list(LEVELS_DB), amplitudes=residual_levels, phases="sine"
)
sound = finish(sound)
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=200, fmax=3500)

A wide band losing half to a narrow one

Listen for the short bursts of hiss between the long ones. A band of noise plays steadily while every 0.6 s a wider band, holding it and the band above it, sounds for a moment; then the same with the upper band steady. The booklet reports the steady band heard going on unbroken through each wide burst, which is heard as a short burst of the other band only: high in the first part, low in the second, although the wide bursts are the same in both.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 34. B, the wide burst, holds 0 to 2 kHz and lasts 200 ms; L holds 0 to 1 kHz and H 1 to 2 kHz, and the one in play fills the 400 ms before and after each B, with no gaps, as in the booklet. The booklet plays the two parts twice in alternation; this reproduction plays each once. Ours: six cycles of each, 5 ms cross-fades, and a pause of 1 s between them.

def captured_band(captor, other, n_cycles=6):
    t = np.arange(round((0.6 * n_cycles + 0.4) * FS)) / FS
    gated = on(t, 0.4 + 0.6 * np.arange(n_cycles), 0.2)
    return so.Sound(captor.data[: len(t), 0] + gated * other.data[: len(t), 0], FS).ramp(10e-3)

Three bands in a cycle

Listen for a busy pattern of hisses. Each cycle of 0.8 s is a high wide burst, a middle band, a low wide burst and the middle band again. The booklet describes the pattern as very complex at first; the three sounds that follow help to pick out three streams in it, which the booklet reports listeners can then hear without help: a high one, a low one and a middle band going on throughout.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 35. Three octave bands, L from 500 to 1000 Hz, M from 1000 to 2000 Hz and H from 2000 to 4000 Hz; A holds H and M, B holds L and M, and the cycle is A, M, B, M. As in the booklet, each band is a sum of equal tones in random phase on the harmonics of 2 Hz that fall in it, which gives sharp edges, and all three have the same spectrum level. The booklet does not give the bursts' length; 200 ms is ours. As on the previous example, the middle band runs on unbroken and the high and low bands are switched on with it, here with 5 ms cross-fades.

cc12_three_bands_in_a_cycle.flac, 4.8 s, mono

Code
def sharp_band(lo, hi, dur, seed):
    """Equal tones in random phase on the harmonics of 2 Hz from `lo` to `hi`: a band of noise
    with sharp edges. Each tone has RMS 1, so every such band has the same spectrum level."""
    harmonics = np.arange(lo // 2, hi // 2 + 1)
    rng = np.random.default_rng(seed)
    snd = so.harmonic_complex(dur, FS, 2, harmonics=harmonics, phases="random", rng=rng, ramp=0)
    return snd * np.sqrt(len(harmonics))


SLOT, LONGEST = 0.2, 9.6
L_BAND = sharp_band(500, 1000, LONGEST, seed=1)
M_BAND = sharp_band(1000, 2000, LONGEST, seed=2)
H_BAND = sharp_band(2000, 4000, LONGEST, seed=3)


def bands(standard=None, n_standard=0, n_full=6):
    """`n_standard` cycles with only the `standard` band ("L", "M" or "H"), then `n_full` of the
    whole pattern."""
    t = np.arange(round(4 * SLOT * (n_standard + n_full) * FS)) / FS
    full = 4 * SLOT * n_standard
    cycles = 4 * SLOT * np.arange(n_standard + n_full)
    before, after = cycles[cycles < full], cycles[cycles >= full]
    high = on(t, after, SLOT) + (standard == "H") * on(t, before, SLOT)
    low = on(t, after + 2 * SLOT, SLOT) + (standard == "L") * on(t, before + 2 * SLOT, SLOT)
    middle = on(t, [full], t[-1] + 1.0) + (standard == "M") * on(t, [0.0], full)
    mix = low * L_BAND.data[: len(t), 0] + middle * M_BAND.data[: len(t), 0] + high * H_BAND.data[: len(t), 0]
    return so.Sound(mix, FS)


sound = finish(bands())
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=300, fmax=5000)

Glides and a vowel

The same capture with sounds that change: glides, where capture tells whether hearing takes the captured part as a continuation of what came before or as a repeat of it, and a vowel, which changes when one of its harmonics is captured.

A glide moving up to match another

Listen for two high glides in a row, A and then B, with the lower glide C under B. When A captures B you hear high glides at twice the rate of the low one; when it does not, a pure A alternates with a richer B-and-C. A starts on B's path, half an octave below, and moves up in steps until it is the same glide as B. The booklet reports capture getting stronger as A approaches B, and best when B is a copy of A.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 36. Pure glides rising at 2.5 octaves a second, 170 ms long, each followed by 30 ms of silence: B rises from 744 to 998 Hz and C, an octave below, from 372 to 499 Hz, always; A starts half an octave below B, where it lies on the same straight path, and ends the same as B, all as in the booklet. Ours: five positions of A (a step of an eighth of an octave), four cycles at each, played once where the booklet plays the series twice, equal levels, and 10 ms ramps.

cc13_a_glide_moving_up_to_match_another.flac, 8.0 s, mono

RATE, B_START, C_START = 2.5, 744, 372


def glides(a_start, n_cycles, gap=0.03, joined=False):
    """`n_cycles` of A, then B with C, each followed by `gap` seconds of silence; with `joined`, A
    and B are one glide with no silence between them."""
    period = 2 * (0.17 + gap)
    events = []
    for k in range(n_cycles):
        t = period * k
        if joined:
            events.append((t, glide(a_start, 0.17 + gap + 0.17, RATE)))
        else:
            events += [(t, glide(a_start, 0.17, RATE)), (t + 0.17 + gap, glide(B_START, 0.17, RATE))]
        events.append((t + 0.17 + gap, glide(C_START, 0.17, RATE)))
    return place(events, dur=period * n_cycles)


positions = B_START * 2 ** np.linspace(-0.5, 0, 5)
sound = finish(so.concat([glides(a, 4) for a in positions]))
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=250, fmax=1500)

A vowel losing part of a harmonic

Listen for the vowel. First four times as "i" (as in hid), then four times without its third harmonic, which the booklet hears as nasal, between "e" (bed) and "en" (bend). Then "i" eight more times, but with its third harmonic sounding on all the time, before, between and after the vowels. The booklet reports that the vowel then shifts toward "en": the steady tone is heard as a sound of its own, so part of the third harmonic is no longer counted in the vowel. It adds that the effect depends on the room, and is best heard over headphones.

Bregman & Ahad, Demonstration 37, after Darwin (1984). A vowel of harmonics of 125 Hz in sine phase, 450 ms long including 50 ms rise and decay, once a second; spectral peaks at 375, 2000, 2500 and 3500 Hz, all as in the booklet. The booklet set the harmonics' levels by ear and does not list them; here they come from four resonances at those peaks, with bandwidths 60, 100, 120 and 175 Hz, on a source falling 6 dB an octave, up to 4000 Hz, and are ours. The booklet's last part, comparisons with the third harmonic turned down by 3 to 18 dB, is left out; the pause of 1 s before the third part, and the third harmonic starting 0.5 s before it, are ours.

cc14_a_vowel_losing_part_of_a_harmonic.flac, 17.2 s, mono

Code
PEAKS, BANDWIDTHS = (375, 2000, 2500, 3500), (60, 100, 120, 175)


def vowel_level(f):
    """Resonances at `PEAKS` (each 1 at 0 Hz) on a source falling 6 dB an octave."""
    gain = 1 / np.maximum(f, 1.0)
    for peak, bw in zip(PEAKS, BANDWIDTHS, strict=True):
        half = bw / 2
        below, above = (f - peak) ** 2 + half**2, (f + peak) ** 2 + half**2
        gain = gain * (peak**2 + half**2) / np.sqrt(below * above)
    return gain


i_onsets, en_onsets, captured_onsets = 0.25 + np.arange(4), 4.25 + np.arange(4), 9.25 + np.arange(8)


def vowel_gate(t, onsets):
    """The vowel's 450 ms with 50 ms rise and decay, at each of `onsets`."""
    return on(t, np.asarray(onsets) + 0.025, 0.4, ramp=0.05)


def vowel_harmonics(t, f, n):
    if n != 3:
        gate = vowel_gate(t, np.concatenate([i_onsets, en_onsets, captured_onsets]))
    else:
        gate = vowel_gate(t, i_onsets) + on(t, [8.75 + 0.025], 8.25 - 0.05, ramp=0.05)
    return vowel_level(f) * gate


harmonics = np.arange(1, 33)
sound = so.harmonic_complex(17.25, FS, 125, harmonics=harmonics, amplitudes=vowel_harmonics, phases="sine")
sound = finish(sound)
fig, playhead = show(sound, fmin=80, fmax=4500)
