Modulation spectrogram

A spectrogram shows how a sound's power spectrum changes over time. A modulation spectrogram does the same for its modulation spectrum: how fast, and how deeply, the envelope of each frequency band rises and falls, moment by moment (Greenberg & Kingsbury, 1997; Kingsbury et al., 1998). It has three axes, time, acoustic frequency and modulation rate, so it is a cube, and a page can only show cuts through it. The main one here is modulation rate against time, pooled over the acoustic bands. The other is acoustic frequency against modulation rate at one moment (Atlas & Shamma, 2003): beside the player it follows the sound as it plays, and under the speech it is drawn at three moments.

This is a different modulation spectrum from the two-dimensional one of rates and spectral densities, measured over a whole sound, that Spectrotemporal ripples defines and Hearing a modulation spectrum turns back into sound.

  • How it is computed: band envelopes, then a bank of windowed complex exponentials on each envelope.
  • A modulation rate that glides: a pattern a spectrogram cannot show.
  • Modulation that glides in frequency: the same picture turned on its side, and rate and frequency gliding together.
  • Speech, babble and noise: the syllable rhythm of each talker, blurred by more voices and gone in noise with the same spectrum.
  • Three textures: crickets, applause and rain, each with its own rates.
  • Three cuts through the cube: one moment, one rate and the pooled view, side by side.
  • What this page leaves out: power, live analysis, inversion and the front end.

Press play and a line follows the sound across every time axis in its plots. Click any time axis to play from that point. Start with your volume low.

To run this page yourself, open it as a notebook in Colab or download it. It is the same code, and it fetches the recordings it needs from the sonore repository.

How it is computed

The sound first goes through 16 bands from 100 Hz to 7 kHz, equally spaced on the ERB scale (so.cosine_filterbank, as on Analysis and resynthesis), and each band's envelope is taken at 1000 Hz (a cochleagram). Each envelope then goes through a bank of modulation filters, one per rate up to 64 Hz. A filter is a complex exponential at its rate under a Hann window, so it measures the envelope's Fourier component at that rate over the window, the way one bin of an STFT does for the sound itself. The same window, without the exponential, measures the envelope's local mean, and the ratio of the two is the modulation depth:

m_{b,k}(t) = \frac{2\,|y_{b,k}(t)|}{\mu_{b,k}(t)}

A band whose envelope is 1 + m \sin(2\pi f_m t) reads m at the rate f_m, so 100% modulation is 0 dB and 50% is -6 dB. Depth says how modulated a band is, whatever its level, which is what makes a quiet band's rhythm as visible as a loud one's. The pooled view adds the bands' modulation power and divides by their summed squared means, so there the louder bands count for more.

The window length is a choice made for the sound at hand, as it is for an STFT. Here every rate shares one window, so the bank is the STFT of each envelope: rates sit on a grid of 1/T Hz for a window of T seconds, starting at two cycles per window. A 1 s window resolves rates 1 Hz apart from 2 Hz up but blurs each change over about half a second; a 0.25 s window places changes four times more precisely in time, with rates 4 Hz apart from 8 Hz up. The examples show both, like a narrowband and a wideband spectrogram. Cells drawn gray are ones the analysis cannot trust: where the window runs past either end of the sound, half a window at each end, the same at every rate. A rate faster than a band is wide can't be trusted either, since a band's envelope can't change faster than that, and is drawn gray too; with 16 bands even the lowest is wider than 64 Hz, so on this page that never happens.

import os
import urllib.request

import matplotlib.pyplot as plt
import numpy as np

import sonore as so

plt.rcParams.update({"font.size": 9, "axes.titlesize": 10, "figure.dpi": 100})


def fetch(path):
    """A file from the sonore repository, by its path there: the local copy when this runs from
    the repository root, otherwise downloaded from GitHub to the same relative path."""
    if not os.path.exists(path):
        os.makedirs(os.path.dirname(path) or ".", exist_ok=True)
        urllib.request.urlretrieve("https://raw.githubusercontent.com/choyun1/sonore/main/" + path, path)
    return path


def finish(snd):
    """How every sound in the gallery is played: 5 ms ramps, RMS 0.1, peak at most 0.95."""
    snd = snd.ramp(5e-3).normalize(rms=0.1)
    return snd.normalize(peak=0.95) if snd.peak > 0.95 else snd
Code
# The two talkers, by the CMU ARCTIC speaker names. Sources: docs/speech/SOURCES.md.
SPEAKERS = {"Male talker": "bdl", "Female talker": "slt"}
talkers = {
    label: finish(so.load(fetch(f"docs/speech/{speaker}_arctic_a0131.flac")))
    for label, speaker in SPEAKERS.items()
}
tracks = {label: so.f0_track(snd) for label, snd in talkers.items()}

fs = 16000
rng = np.random.default_rng(0)


def analyze(snd):
    """The cochleagram, and modulation spectrograms with a 1 s and a 0.25 s window."""
    env = so.cosine_filterbank(n_bands=16, f_lo=100, f_hi=7000).analyze(snd).envelopes(fs=1000)
    return env, so.ModulationSpectrogram(env, window=1.0), so.ModulationSpectrogram(env, window=0.25)


def depth_db(msg):
    """Depth in dB for every band, rate and time window, NaN where the cell is not valid."""
    db = 20 * np.log10(msg.depth[0])
    db[~msg.valid] = np.nan
    return db


def rate_ticks(axis):
    """Label a logarithmic rate axis at whole octaves (2, 4, ..., 64 Hz) and nowhere else."""
    from matplotlib.ticker import FixedLocator, NullFormatter, ScalarFormatter

    axis.set_major_locator(FixedLocator([2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64]))
    axis.set_major_formatter(ScalarFormatter())
    axis.set_minor_formatter(NullFormatter())


def draw(column, snd, marks):
    """The cochleagram and both modulation spectrograms, one above the other in ``column`` (a
    figure or subfigure), with dashed lines at ``marks`` [s]. Returns the analyses and axes."""
    env, msg, sharp = analyze(snd)
    axes = column.subplots(3, 1, sharex=True)
    env.plot(ax=axes[0])
    axes[0].set(title="Cochleagram", xlabel="")
    msg.plot(ax=axes[1], colorbar=False)
    axes[1].set(title="Modulation spectrogram, 1 s window (pooled over bands)", xlabel="")
    sharp.plot(ax=axes[2], colorbar=False)
    axes[2].set_title("Modulation spectrogram, 0.25 s window (pooled over bands)")
    # One color scale for both windows, so their depths can be compared
    top = max(np.nanmax(20 * np.log10(view.pooled_depth()[0])) for view in (msg, sharp))
    for ax in axes[1:]:
        ax.collections[0].set_clim(top - 30, top)
    column.colorbar(axes[1].collections[0], ax=axes[1:], label="Depth [dB]", shrink=0.9)
    for ax in axes[1:]:
        rate_ticks(ax.yaxis)
    for ax in axes:
        for m in marks:
            ax.axvline(m, color="c", lw=1, ls="--")
    return msg, list(axes)


def show(snd, marks=(), start=None):
    """``draw`` for one sound, and the band x rate depth image (1 s window) that follows the sound
    beside the player."""
    fig = plt.figure(figsize=(9, 8.4), layout="constrained")
    msg, axes = draw(fig, snd, marks)
    db = depth_db(msg)
    top = np.nanmax(db)
    # Only the time windows that lie wholly inside the sound: before the first one and after the
    # last, the image holds still. And only rates about half an octave apart, so the image's
    # even grid of cells reads as a logarithmic rate axis, as in the figures.
    measured = msg.valid.any(axis=(0, 1))
    columns = np.unique([np.argmin(np.abs(np.log(msg.fm / (2 * 2 ** (k / 2))))) for k in range(11)])
    live = {
        "t": msg.t[measured],
        "image": db[:, columns][:, :, measured],
        "x": msg.fm[columns],
        "y": msg.f,
        "range": (top - 30, top),
        "xlabel": "Modulation rate [Hz]",
        "ylabel": "Frequency [Hz]",
        "title": "Depth",
        "start": snd.duration / 2 if start is None else start,
    }
    return fig, axes, live


def show_pair(sounds, marks, moments):
    """One column per talker: ``draw``, and under it the band x rate depth image (1 s window) at
    each of that talker's ``moments`` [s], named by their keys. Returns the figure and, for
    each talker, the panels the playhead follows."""
    fig = plt.figure(figsize=(12, 11), layout="constrained")
    columns = fig.subfigures(1, 2)
    playhead = {}
    for column, (label, snd) in zip(columns, sounds.items(), strict=True):
        upper, lower = column.subfigures(2, 1, height_ratios=[3, 1])
        msg, axes = draw(upper, snd, marks[label])
        upper.suptitle(label, fontsize=11)
        db = depth_db(msg)
        top = np.nanmax(db)
        cuts = lower.subplots(1, len(moments[label]), sharey=True)
        for ax, (name, moment) in zip(cuts, moments[label].items(), strict=True):
            i = int(np.argmin(np.abs(msg.t - moment)))
            image = ax.pcolormesh(
                msg.fm, msg.f, db[:, :, i], cmap="magma", vmin=top - 30, vmax=top, shading="nearest"
            )
            image.cmap.set_bad("0.75")
            ax.set(xscale="log", yscale="log", title=f"{name}, {msg.t[i]:.1f} s", xlabel="Rate [Hz]")
            rate_ticks(ax.xaxis)
        cuts[0].set_ylabel("Frequency [Hz]")
        lower.colorbar(image, ax=cuts, label="Depth [dB]", shrink=0.9)
        playhead[label] = axes
    return fig, playhead

Steady noise reads about -26 dB at 2 Hz and -27 dB at 64 Hz: nearly the same depth at every rate. In one band the depth scatters by about 5.5 dB (standard deviation) from moment to moment; pooled over the bands it scatters by about 1.1 dB. That is why the pooled plots are smooth and the band x rate images grainy. Every filter of the 1 s window is 1.4 Hz wide at half power, whatever its rate, as every bin of an STFT is equally wide. The filters of the 0.25 s window are four times wider and take in more of the envelope's random fluctuation, so on steady noise they read about 6 dB deeper; that is why the 0.25 s panels look brighter throughout. The modulation filters of a model of modulation detection (Dau et al., 1997) instead widen with their rate, with a Q of 2 above 10 Hz; sonore's bank can do that too (see What this page leaves out).

Beside each player the band x rate image follows the sound: press play, or click a time axis. Before that it shows a moment chosen for each example. In the first and last half second, where the window would run past the sound, it holds the nearest moment that was measured.

A modulation rate that glides

Noise whose amplitude is modulated at a rate that rises smoothly from 2 to 32 Hz over 8 s, at 50% depth throughout. The modulation is applied to the whole noise, so every band carries it.

A gliding modulation rate

You hear a slow throb speed up into a flutter and then a rough buzz. The cochleagram is a blur of noise, but both modulation spectrograms show the rate climbing. The 1 s window draws it as a thin line from 2 Hz up, its rows 1 Hz apart. The 0.25 s window starts at 8 Hz, its slowest rate, and its rows are 4 Hz apart, so the line is wider and climbs in steps. Next to the player, every band lights up at the same rate at once: the modulation is common to all frequencies.

g1_a_gliding_modulation_rate.flac, 8.0 s, mono

Code
dur, r0, r1 = 8.0, 2.0, 32.0
t = np.arange(int(dur * fs)) / fs
phase = 2 * np.pi * r0 * dur / np.log(r1 / r0) * ((r1 / r0) ** (t / dur) - 1)
glide = (1 + 0.5 * np.sin(phase)) * rng.standard_normal(len(t))
sound = finish(so.Sound(glide, fs))
fig, playhead, live = show(sound)

Modulation that glides in frequency

The gliding rate lights a vertical strip in the band x rate image: one rate, every band. Its counterpart on the other axis is a horizontal strip, every rate in one band, and here it glides up in frequency. The sound is a train of 5 ms noise bursts at random times, 12 a second on average. Random timing modulates the envelope at every rate at once. The bursts are confined to a band a third of an octave wide whose center rises from 200 Hz to 5 kHz over 8 s, and a steady noise 30 dB below them fills the other bands. in_band does the confining with a mask on an STFT (64 ms windows), whose Gaussian gain on a log-frequency axis follows the band's center.

Code
glide_rng = np.random.default_rng(1)  # the glides' own draws, so the examples below stay as they were


def log_glide(start, end, duration, t):
    """A value gliding from ``start`` to ``end`` at a constant rate in octaves, at times ``t`` [s]."""
    return start * (end / start) ** (t / duration)


def in_band(snd, center, width=1 / 3, background_db=-30):
    """``snd`` kept only in a band ``width`` octaves wide (at half height) around ``center(t)``
    [Hz], plus steady noise ``background_db`` below it."""
    stft = so.STFT(snd, win_dur=0.064, hop_dur=0.008)
    octaves = np.log2(np.maximum(stft.f, 1.0)[:, None] / center(stft.t)[None, :])
    gains = np.exp(-0.5 * (octaves / (width / 2.355)) ** 2)  # 2.355 SDs make the width at half height
    band = (stft * so.Mask(gains[None], stft)).to_sound()
    background = so.Sound(glide_rng.standard_normal(len(snd)), snd.fs)
    return band + background.normalize(rms=band.rms * 10 ** (background_db / 20))

Bursts in a band that glides up

You hear a crackle rise in pitch. In the cochleagram each burst is a short mark, and the marks climb. Next to the player a horizontal strip climbs with them: one or two bands deeply modulated at every rate, the rest at the depth of steady noise. The pooled views are bright at every rate throughout, since some band always carries the bursts.

g2_bursts_in_a_band_that_glides_up.flac, 8.0 s, mono

Code
dur = 8.0
t = np.arange(int(dur * fs)) / fs
burst = np.hanning(int(0.005 * fs))
train = np.zeros(len(t))
for start in np.cumsum(glide_rng.exponential(1 / 12, size=200)):
    i = int(start * fs)
    if i + len(burst) <= len(t):
        train[i : i + len(burst)] += burst
bursts = so.Sound(train * glide_rng.standard_normal(len(t)), fs)
sound = finish(in_band(bursts, lambda times: log_glide(200, 5000, dur, times)))
fig, playhead, live = show(sound)

Now both at once: noise fully modulated at one rate, confined to a band, with the rate and the band's center each gliding up or down. The four combinations follow one another, 4 s each: rate up and frequency up, rate up and frequency down, rate down and frequency up, and both down. The rate glides between 2 and 32 Hz, the center between 500 Hz and 4 kHz.

Rate and frequency gliding together

Next to the player a single spot moves diagonally: up and to the right, down and to the right, up and to the left, then down and to the left. In the pooled views the rate draws a rising and a falling line in each half. A band also lights up and goes dark as the noise passes through it, which is itself a slow modulation, so the bands the spot has just crossed glow at the slowest rates. The dashed lines mark the joins.

g3_rate_and_frequency_gliding_together.flac, 16.0 s, mono

Code
dur = 4.0
t = np.arange(int(dur * fs)) / fs
parts = []
for r0, r1 in ((2, 32), (32, 2)):  # rate up, then down
    for f0, f1 in ((500, 4000), (4000, 500)):  # center up, then down

Speech, babble and noise

For each talker (Two talkers): the sentence from the CMU ARCTIC corpus (Kominek & Black, 2004), then 4 s of babble, then 4 s of noise with the sentence's long-term spectrum. The collection has one sentence by the female talker, so each talker's babble is that talker's own sentence six times over, each copy repeated end to end and started at a random point. Speech is modulated most strongly at a few hertz, the rate of its syllables (Greenberg & Kingsbury, 1997). That rhythm is what babble partly hides and speech-shaped noise (Tones and noises) lacks entirely. Hearing a modulation spectrum measures the male talker's sentence as a whole, and hears what is left when its modulation keeps its rates but loses its timing (mt2).

Code
n = int(4 * fs)
mixes, segments = {}, {}
for label, snd in talkers.items():
    x = snd.data[:, 0]
    repeated = np.tile(x, n // len(x) + 2)
    babble = np.zeros(n)
    for start in rng.integers(len(x), size=6):
        babble += repeated[start : start + n]
    ssn = so.long_term_spectrum([snd]).to_sound(4, fs, rng=0)
    parts = [snd, so.Sound(babble, fs).normalize(rms=0.1), ssn.normalize(rms=0.1)]
    mixes[label] = finish(so.concat(parts))
    ends = np.cumsum([p.duration for p in parts])
    segments[label] = {"Speech": (0, ends[0]), "Babble": (ends[0], ends[1]), "Noise": (ends[1], ends[2])}

Speech, babble and noise

The dashed lines mark where the babble and the noise begin; under the modulation spectrograms is the band x rate image at one moment of each part. Under the sentence the pooled depth is high from 2 to 8 Hz. In the babble it is several dB lower there and more even across rates, since the six copies' syllables fill each other's gaps. In the speech-shaped noise it falls further and is nearly even across rates, as steady noise's is (measured above); above 16 Hz the noise is more deeply modulated than the speech. The bright patch just before the first dashed line is the end of the sentence running into the babble, which windows that span both read as modulation.

The two talkers look much alike. During the sentence their pooled depths differ by 2 dB or less at every rate printed. The rhythm is set by the syllables, and the two talkers read the same syllables at nearly the same pace. Pitch does not show at all: the fastest rate here, 64 Hz, is below either talker's lowest F0 (printed above), so the beating of harmonics within a band, at the rate of F0, is beyond the top of the plots. The difference between the voices is in a spectrogram (Two talkers), not here.

Three textures

Crickets, applause and rain, 3.5 s of each, from the recordings on the Sound textures page (crickets, applause, rain). A texture is a sound whose statistics stay put, so its modulation spectrogram should hold still while each texture lasts and change at the joins.

    deepest[name] = msg.fm[np.nanargmax(median)]
    print(
        f"{name:9} deepest at {msg.fm[np.nanargmax(median)]:4.3g} Hz ({np.nanmax(median):5.1f} dB),",
        f"median over 2 to 64 Hz {np.nanmedian(median[msg.fm >= 2]):5.1f} dB",
    )

Crickets, applause and rain

The printout gives each texture's pooled depth, median over its middle 2.5 s. The crickets chirp in fast pulses, and their modulation sits at the top of the rate axis (deepest at 46 Hz) and hardly anywhere else. Applause is a crowd of claps and adds a band of slow modulation, deepest at 3 Hz. Rain has many small drops at no particular rate: no rate stands out, and its depth is nearly the same at every rate, as steady noise's is. The 1 s window separates the rates more cleanly, since nothing here changes quickly; the 0.25 s window marks the joins more sharply. Three cuts through the cube

msg.slices(t, rate) draws three cuts through the cube at once, on one color scale: rate against time pooled over bands, frequency against time at one rate, and frequency against rate at one moment. Here they are for each talker's speech, babble and noise from above, at 4 Hz and at a moment in the sentence.

Three cuts, male talker

The male talker's sentence, babble and noise from above, cut at 4 Hz and at 1.3 s. The middle panel shows which bands carry the 4 Hz rhythm and when: during the sentence, nearly all of them, in step with the syllables, since a syllable's onset raises the level across the spectrum; in the babble, still many, but no longer in step with each other; in the noise, almost none. On the right, the moment at 1.3 s: depth is high from 2 Hz to about 10 Hz in most bands and falls off above that.

What this page leaves out

  • Windows that scale with the rate. so.ModulationSpectrogram(env) without window= gives each rate a window of three of its own cycles (cycles=3), rates half an octave apart from 0.5 Hz: 6 s at 0.5 Hz, 47 ms at 64 Hz. Slow rates are then resolved finely and fast ones placed sharply in time, like a wavelet scalogram, but the gray edges are as wide as half of each rate's window, so on a short sound the trusted cells form a U.
  • Power. Each cell also has a modulation power, |2y|^2, which says how much of the sound a modulation is rather than how modulated a band is (msg.power, and msg.average() over time).
  • Live analysis. With align="causal" every window ends at the time it reports, as a live analysis would see the sound, so each number comes half a window later than in the centered analysis used here.
  • Inversion. The phase of each filter output is dropped and the local mean divided out, so the modulation spectrogram can't be turned back into sound. To change a sound's modulation, filter its envelopes and resynthesize, as on the Analysis and resynthesis page.
  • The front end. Any filterbank's envelopes will do, a gammatone bank's for instance, and the envelopes can be compressed first; this page keeps one front end throughout.
