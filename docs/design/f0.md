# F0 tracking

The design of an F0 tracker for sonore: candidates, refinement by
instantaneous frequency, a periodicity score, and a tracking pass that
decides voicing. It is the first step of the WORLD-style analysis and
synthesis on the README roadmap. WORLD's estimators (DIO, Harvest, and the
StoneMask refinement) are the reference for what a good track is; the
design keeps their refinement but not their candidate stage, for reasons
measured below. The tracker is meant to be compared with the classic
cepstral F0 that `so.Cepstrum.f0` already provides (`cepstrum.md`, C4 and
C6), and every number below reports that baseline beside it.

Status: accepted 2026-10-01, with decisions D1–D9 as recommended below
(C9, the laryngograph reference, was added the same day). Implemented in
`src/sonore/analysis/f0.py` (`so.f0_track`, `F0Track`) with one addition the
laryngograph check called for, the subharmonic rule of C10; tested in
`tests/analysis/test_f0.py`.

## How the claims are verified

As in `cepstrum.md`, each claim is numbered and tagged:

- **[proof]**: a short argument given here.
- **[check]**: a number printed by `tools/check_f0_claims.py`. The script
  uses only NumPy, SciPy and soundfile, shares no code with sonore, and
  holds a small prototype of the tracker described here so that the numbers
  can be reproduced. It runs in about 20 s. The numbers come from NumPy
  2.4.6 and SciPy 1.17.1.
- **[world]**: a number printed by `tools/crosscheck_f0_world.py`, which runs
  WORLD's Harvest and DIO + StoneMask through `pyworld` 0.3.5 on the same
  signals. `pyworld` (MIT licence) is a comparison oracle only, never a
  dependency, and no WORLD code was read or copied for the prototype.
- **[fda]**: a number printed by `tools/check_f0_fda.py` on the FDA
  evaluation database (Bagshaw; CSTR, University of Edinburgh), which Cho
  supplied on 2026-10-01. It states no licence, so it is kept in the
  project's shared files and not in this repository; the script takes its
  folder as an argument. It runs in about 5 minutes with `pyworld`.
- **[source]**: a published result (see References).

## Words used here

A **time window** in this document is a short stretch of sound around one
point in time, every 5 ms, at which the tracker reports an F0 (or
"unvoiced"). It is not a frame in sonore's sense (`Frame`, an invertible
analysis); see the vocabulary note in philosophy.md. A time window is **voiced** when the tracker reports an F0 there, and
**unvoiced** when it reports none (silence, noise, fricatives).

## Test material

- **Synthetic vowels.** Harmonics of a known F0 contour, each with the gain
  of a four-formant /a/ (the filter of `cepstrum.md`) at its instantaneous
  frequency, 16 kHz, 1 s: steady 80, 120, 200 and 350 Hz; a glide from 100
  to 200 Hz at one octave per second; a 150 Hz vibrato of ±6% at 5.5 Hz.
  The /a/ has F1 at 730 Hz, so its fundamental is weak, as in most
  speech. Variants: white noise at 20, 10 and 0 dB SNR, and a high-pass at
  300 Hz (telephone band, no fundamental).
- **The gallery sentence** `bdl_arctic_a0131` (2.53 s, male, F0 about
  95–150 Hz) with two references, neither of them ground truth: the stored
  Harvest track (`docs/speech/SOURCES.md`) and the corpus's own pitch marks
  (`bdl_arctic_a0131.pm`). The marks run on through unvoiced stretches as
  runs of identical intervals; the checker treats runs of three or more
  identical intervals slower than 110 Hz as unvoiced, which leaves 377
  time windows with a mark-based F0.
- **Two held-out sentences**, `rms_arctic_a0132` and `a0133` (male, F0
  about 70–155 Hz), against Harvest only. The prototype's settings were
  chosen on the synthetic set and on `bdl`, not on these.

The error measures follow Harvest's paper (Morise, 2017): a time window is a
**gross error** if it is called unvoiced or is more than 20% off; the
**fine error** is the deviation on the other time windows. Time windows within 50 ms
of either end are excluded.

## What WORLD does

From the Harvest paper (Morise, 2017; read through the ISCA archive) and
the DIO paper's abstract (Morise, Kawahara & Katayose, 2009):

- **Candidates from fundamental components.** A band-pass filter whose
  band holds only the fundamental outputs a near-sinusoid. Its upward and
  downward zero crossings, peaks and dips give four interval estimates that
  agree; their mean is a candidate. Harvest uses Nuttall-windowed cosines
  spanning four periods of the centre frequency, 40 channels per octave,
  and keeps a channel's candidate only if it lies within ±10% of the
  centre. DIO uses low-pass filters in the same way.
- **Refinement by instantaneous frequency.** Each candidate is refined from
  the instantaneous frequencies of its first six harmonics, weighted by
  their power (Flanagan's formula), and scored by how well they agree.
  StoneMask does the same for DIO.
- **Connection rules.** Candidates are joined into contours by rules on
  the change from one time window to the next, short voiced runs are dropped, voiced regions are
  extended across gaps, and each voiced section is smoothed (a zero-phase
  second-order Butterworth at 30 Hz). Harvest's stated aim is to cut the
  errors where voiced time windows are called unvoiced.

The exact thresholds were read from the paper through a summarizing fetch
and are not quoted here as verified.

## The design

Four stages, each a few lines of NumPy in the prototype:

1. **Candidates.** Up to four per 5 ms time window, from the local minima below
   0.5 of YIN's cumulative-mean-normalized difference function over a
   25 ms window (de Cheveigné & Kawahara, 2002), each refined by a parabola.
   The checker also implements Harvest's filter-bank candidates (24 channels
   per octave) for comparison (D1).
2. **Refinement.** For a candidate f at time t: a Blackman window three
   periods of f long centred at t; the DTFT of the windowed segment at
   k·f, k = 1..6, and of the same segment one sample later. The phase
   advance of harmonic k over that sample is its instantaneous frequency
   IF_k. The new F0 is Σ_k |X_k|² IF_k / k divided by Σ_k |X_k|², repeated
   twice. This is the StoneMask idea written from its description, not
   from its code.
3. **Score.** The normalized correlation between a stretch three periods
   long and the same stretch one refined period later: 1 for a periodic
   sound, near 0 for noise (C3).
4. **Tracking and voicing.** A Viterbi pass over the states (unvoiced,
   candidate 1, …) of every time window. A candidate costs 1 − score and the
   unvoiced state costs 1 − θ (θ = 0.5), so a time window prefers voicing when
   its best score exceeds θ. Moving between candidates costs 2 per octave
   of change, and a voicing change costs 0.5. This is the dynamic
   programming of RAPT (Talkin, 1995) and pYIN (Mauch & Dixon, 2014) in
   its simplest form, in place of Harvest's connection rules (D4).

## Claims

**C1. Filter-bank candidates need the fundamental; the difference function
does not.** [check] On the clean vibrato both candidate stages put a
candidate within 5% of the true F0 in every time window. In white noise at 10 dB
SNR, the filter bank does so in 57% of time windows and the difference function
in 100%: the /a/ filter's gain at 150 Hz is 26 dB below its gain near F1,
so noise in the lowest band swamps the fundamental first. High-passed at 300 Hz, the bank
finds the F0 in no time window and the difference function in every time window. The
bank also offers every isolated harmonic as a candidate: on a steady
120 Hz vowel the channels at 120, 240 and 360 Hz output 120, 239.4 and
360.1 Hz, all within their ±10% gate, so the later stages must reject
octave-up candidates whichever stage is used.

**C2. Refinement by instantaneous frequency gains a factor of 10 or more
over the candidate's own estimate.** [proof, check] For a stationary
sinusoid at f0, the phase of a fixed-window DTFT advances by exactly
2π f0 / fs per sample whatever the analysis frequency, so IF_k / k is
exact up to leakage from the neighbouring harmonics, which the Blackman
window's sidelobes (below −58 dB) keep small. Median error before and
after refinement:

| Signal | Parabolic candidate | Refined | Largest refined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Steady 80 Hz | 0.0012% | 4e-7% | 2e-6% |
| Steady 120 Hz | 0.0036% | 1e-8% | 1e-7% |
| Steady 200 Hz | 0.0076% | 1e-6% | 1e-6% |
| Steady 350 Hz | 0.012% | 0.0009% | 0.005% |
| Glide, 1 oct/s | 0.25% | 0.015% | 0.12% |
| Vibrato ±6%, 5.5 Hz | 0.48% | 0.036% | 0.12% |

The remaining error on the moving contours comes from F0 changing within
the window; 0.12% is 2 cents. Why the 350 Hz case is less exact than the
others was not investigated; it is still 1000 times better than needed.

**C3. The score of a periodic sound in independent white noise is
s / (1 + s), s the power SNR, but only with an exact fractional shift.**
[proof, check] For x = p + n with p periodic, the correlation of x with
x shifted by one period is P_p over P_p + P_n in expectation, which is
s / (1 + s). So θ = 0.5 means "the periodic part has at least the power of
the noise". The prototype shifts by linear interpolation, which scales the
shifted copy's white-noise power by g = (1 − φ)² + φ² for a fractional
shift φ, and the score becomes s / sqrt((1 + s)(s + g)). At 120 Hz
(φ = 1/3, g = 0.56) the checker measures 0.992, 0.929, 0.566 and 0.293 at
20, 10, 0 and −5 dB SNR, against 0.992, 0.928, 0.567 and 0.295 predicted
with the interpolation and 0.990, 0.909, 0.500 and 0.240 without it. The
bias depends on φ and so on F0, which is why D3 asks for an exact shift.
White noise alone produces no candidate at all (no difference-function
minimum below 0.5), so its score is 0 in every time window.

**C4. Tracking mainly removes voicing flicker.** [check] On the sentence,
taking the best-scoring candidate in each time window gives 36 voicing switches
and 2 time windows more than 20% off Harvest among the time windows both call voiced;
the Viterbi pass gives 16 switches and none. On the vibrato at 5 dB SNR
neither has a gross error. Octave errors are rare before tracking because
the score already penalises octave-up candidates (half the period
correlates poorly) and the difference function rarely offers octave-down
ones.

**C5. On the synthetic set the tracker is about 10 times more precise than
cepstral F0 and as precise as Harvest, and it keeps working without the
fundamental.** [check, world] Gross error rate / median fine error:

**C6. White noise alone is called unvoiced.** [check, world] One second
of white noise: the tracker voices 0% of time windows, the cepstral baseline 2%,
Harvest 4.5% and DIO 0%.

**C7. On the sentence, the tracker agrees with Harvest wherever both call
the time window voiced, but voices fewer time windows.** [check] Harvest calls 432
time windows voiced.

| | Time windows voiced | Harvest-voiced it voices | Harvest-unvoiced it voices | Both voiced, within 5% of Harvest |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tracker | 341 | 79% | 0% | 99.4% |
| Tracker, bank candidates | 349 | 80% | 5.5% | 99.4% |
| Cepstral baseline | 329 | 75% | 9.6% | 93% |

55 of Harvest's voiced time windows lie inside the marks' unvoiced runs, for
example 0.57–0.67 s, where the marks are a uniform 92 Hz filler and the
prototype finds no periodicity. That stretch is probably the /h/ of "had"
(inferred from the text and timing, not checked by listening). On the
held-out `rms` sentences [world] the gap is wider: the tracker voices 49%
and 58% of Harvest's voiced time windows and none of its unvoiced ones, and
agrees within 5% on 100% and 99.8% of the time windows both voice; the cepstral
baseline voices 51% and 58%, 21% and 16% of Harvest's unvoiced time windows,
and agrees on 85% and 87%. The Harvest-voiced time windows the tracker leaves
out there come in long runs (up to 245 and 240 ms), 15 and 12 dB below the
median level of the time windows it voices, at a lower Harvest F0 (median 84 Hz
against 102 and 103 Hz). That looks like weak or creaky voicing, perhaps at
phrase ends (inferred, not checked by listening). Whether those time windows
are voiced cannot be settled without an
independent reference (D8).

**C9. Against a laryngograph reference, the tracker makes a quarter to
a fifth of Harvest's voicing errors; Harvest misses almost no voiced time window
but voices about a third of the unvoiced ones.** [fda] The FDA database
has 50 sentences each from a male (rl, F0 median 121 Hz) and a female
speaker (sb, 253 Hz), speech and laryngograph at 20 kHz, and an F0 contour
from the laryngograph's cycle marks, with breaks where the voice stops.
A time window is reference-voiced inside a contour segment; time windows within 10 ms
of a segment's ends are left out of the voicing counts. Speech is
resampled to 16 kHz; Harvest gives the same numbers at the native 20 kHz.
The **voicing error** is the share of all scored time windows whose voicing is
wrong.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| Tracker, θ = 0.4 | 3.0% | 0.9% | 1.6% | 8.6% | 1.86% | 93.5% |
| Tracker, all minima, θ = 0.5 | 3.1% | 0.6% | 1.5% | 9.3% | 1.84% | 93.8% |
| Cepstral baseline | 4.8% | 6.1% | 5.7% | 12.1% | 2.96% | 90.8% |
| Harvest | 0.1% | 31.5% | 20.8% | 1.4% | 0.98% | 93.1% |
| DIO + StoneMask | 1.7% | 3.2% | 2.7% | 4.9% | 0.75% | 94.2% |

"All minima" offers every difference-function minimum below 1 as a
candidate (rather than those below 0.5; a minimum above 1 means the sound
is less alike at that lag than on average), so that only the score decides voicing.
Harvest's gross error rate here, 1.9% and 1.4%, is close to the 1.61% its
paper reports on an English database of 2 speakers and 100 items at
20 kHz, which is very likely this one (Morise, 2017; the match is
inferred from that description). That agreement suggests the reference is
being read as Harvest's author read it.

Three readings. First, Harvest's low gross error rate comes from counting
only reference-voiced time windows: a method that voices everything cannot be
penalised by it. Counting both kinds of voicing error, Harvest is wrong on
about 21% of time windows, against 5.8% and 1.5% for the tracker; the extra
time windows it voices are mostly in the unvoiced gaps between voiced stretches
(inferred from its gap-bridging rule, not inspected time window by time window).
Second, where both voice, the tracker is the most precise on the male
voice (98.1% within 5%) and as precise as the others on the female voice,
but makes more octave-type errors on the female voice than Harvest and
DIO (1.8% against about 1%), which the implementation should look at.
Third, the tracker's missed voiced time windows on rl are not only at voicing
edges: of the 1280 missed scored time windows, 58% lie within 50 ms of a
reference boundary, half have no candidate at all from the difference
function, and their reference F0 is lower than the speaker's median
(median 105 Hz against 121 Hz), which looks like low, irregular voicing.
(These last numbers come from a one-off analysis in the thread, not from
the script.)

**C10. Never choosing a candidate whose octave above scores as well
removes most octave-down errors.** [fda] Anything periodic with period T is
also periodic with period 2T, so a candidate at F0/2 can score as high as
the true F0; on the female voice most of the prototype's 1.8% gross errors
where both voice were octave-down (162 time windows, against 29 octave-up). The
library therefore never chooses a candidate f when another candidate within
5% of 2f scores at least as well, less 0.05. With that rule, `so.f0_track`
on the FDA database:

| | Voicing error | >20% off where both voice | Within 5% where both voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| rl (male) | 5.5% | 0.12% | 98.1% |
| sb (female) | 1.6% | 0.54% | 95.2% |

The female voice's gross errors drop below Harvest's (0.98%) and DIO's
(0.75%), and the male voice is unchanged within 0.2 points. The margin
hardly matters: 0.02, 0.05 and 0.1 gave the same numbers within 0.1 points.
A softer alternative, a cost per octave below the time window's highest
candidate (Praat's "octave cost" idea, Boersma 1993), traded fewer female
octave errors for more missed male voicing (0.05 per octave: 0.56% and a
6.0% voicing error on rl), so it was not used.
The margin is the argument `subharmonic_margin` (default 0.05); `None`
turns the rule off, to show the raw choice (Cho, 2026-10-02).

*Amended 2026-10-02 (`female-voices.md`).* On perfectly steady synthetic
vowels from 250 Hz up, the tracker returned F0/3 or F0/5. Such a sound
has a difference-function minimum at every multiple of its period, all
about equally deep, so the four candidates kept could all be multiples,
and a candidate at F0/3 has no candidate at the octave above it. The
library now keeps eight candidates and applies the rule to every whole
multiple (2f, 3f, ...), not only the octave. On the FDA database:

| | Voicing error | >20% off where both voice | Within 5% where both voice |
|---|---|---|---|
| rl (male) | 5.6% | 0.12% | 98.1% |
| sb (female) | 1.5% | 0.51% | 95.2% |

The male voice's voicing error rises by 0.09 points and the female voice's
falls by 0.05. Tracking takes about twice as long (172 s against 82 s for
the 100 sentences), because twice as many candidates are refined and
scored.

## Decisions

**D1. Candidate stage: the difference function (recommended), not
Harvest's filter bank. (accepted 2026-10-01)** The two belong to different families. WORLD's estimators
are interval-based: they time zero crossings and peaks of filtered
signals. YIN belongs to the autocorrelation (ACF) family: its abstract
calls it "based on the well-known autocorrelation method with a number of
modifications that combine to prevent errors" (PubMed 12002874). Its
difference function is d(τ) = Σ (x_j − x_{j+τ})², which for a steady
signal equals 2(r(0) − r(τ)), r the ACF, and the cumulative-mean
normalization removes the ACF's bias towards τ = 0. So the choice is
between WORLD's interval idea and the ACF idea for the first guesses,
with WORLD's refinement either way. The bank needs the fundamental to be audible
above the noise in its own band (C1), and fails outright on telephone-band
speech, where Harvest and DIO fail too (C5). With the difference function,
the same refinement, score and tracking match Harvest's precision on clean
signals and beat it in noise. The cost is that sonore's tracker is not
Harvest; it is a WORLD-style tracker in its refinement only. Alternative:
both stages, with the union of their candidates (more work per time window, and
C1 shows the bank adds nothing the difference function lacks on this set).

**D2. Refinement as in the design, always on. (accepted 2026-10-01)** Six harmonics, a Blackman
window three periods long, two iterations (C2). The three-period window
matches `TVGaborFrame.pitch_adaptive` (`frames.md`, D15) and CheapTrick.

**D3. The score uses an exact fractional shift (recommended). (accepted 2026-10-01)** The shift
by one period is done in the frequency domain (a linear phase over a
zero-padded FFT of the stretch) or with a windowed sinc, so θ has the
meaning C3 gives it at every F0. Boersma (1993) addressed the same
problem for the autocorrelation with sinc interpolation (cited from
memory). Alternative: keep linear interpolation and accept an F0-dependent
bias of up to 0.07 at 0 dB SNR.

**D4. Tracking by Viterbi with three stated costs (recommended), not
Harvest's connection rules. (accepted 2026-10-01)** The costs (θ = 0.5, 2 per octave, 0.5 per
voicing change) are parameters with a meaning, and C4 shows the pass does
what is asked of it. No smoothing filter afterwards: the refined track is
already smooth where the voice is (C2), and smoothing would blur real
jumps. A `smooth=` option can come later if a use needs it.

**D5. Voicing is strict by default, and only the score decides it
(recommended). (accepted 2026-10-01)** A time window is voiced when its periodic part is at least as
strong as the rest (θ = 0.5, C3). Against the laryngograph (C9) this makes
a quarter to a fifth of Harvest's voicing errors and fewer than DIO's,
while noise is never voiced (C6). The candidate stage offers every
difference-function minimum below 1, so that the threshold on the score is the
one voicing rule (C9's "all minima" rows; they lower the male voice's
voicing error from 5.8% to 5.3% and leave the female voice's at 1.5%).
θ stays a parameter: θ = 0.4 trades a few more false voicings for fewer
misses (4.5% voicing error on rl with all minima), and a user who
resynthesizes speech, where a missed voiced time window is an audible
buzz-to-hiss switch, may want it lower still. Not recommended:
Harvest-style extension of voiced runs across gaps, which is where its
21% voicing error comes from. Alternative: θ = 0.4 as the default, which
did best on the male voice here; it loses the plain meaning of C3, and
choosing it on the only reference we have would be tuning to the test.

**D6. API: one function and a small result type (recommended). (accepted 2026-10-01)** A new
module `sonore/analysis/f0.py` with `so.f0_track(sound, f_lo=60, f_hi=500,
hop=0.005, threshold=0.5)` returning an `F0Track` with `t`, `f0` (0 where
unvoiced, one row per channel), `score`, `voiced`, and `plot(ax=None)`.
Its `t` and `f0` go straight into `TVGaborFrame.pitch_adaptive` and
`Cepstrum.lifter`, so the track drives the representations sonore already
has. The candidates and their scores are kept on the result for plotting
and teaching. Alternative: a class with an `analyze` method like the
frames; there is nothing to invert here, so a function is plainer.

**D7. Defaults: 60–500 Hz, 5 ms hop (recommended). (accepted 2026-10-01)** 60 Hz covers the
lowest male voices here (the `rms` sentences reach 70 Hz), 500 Hz covers
children and most speaking voices, and 5 ms is Harvest's hop and
the stored track's. Singing needs `f_hi` up to about 1000 Hz; that is a
parameter, not a different default. Alternative: `pyworld`'s Harvest
defaults, 71–800 Hz.

**D8. The laryngograph reference stays outside the repo (recommended). (accepted 2026-10-01)**
Cho found no EGG channel in the CMU ARCTIC files and supplied the FDA
database instead (C9). With no stated licence, it lives in the project's
shared files, `tools/check_f0_fda.py` reads it from a path, and the
library's tests use only synthetic signals and the repo's own sentence. If
a test against a laryngograph reference is wanted in CI later, PTDB-TUG
(TU Graz; Open Database Licence, with 20 speakers) is the candidate,
pending a read of its licence terms.

**D9. Speed target and implementation. (accepted 2026-10-01)** The library
version tracks the 2.53 s sentence in about 1.1 s, faster than real time.
The prototype takes about 1.5 s
for the 2.5 s sentence in Python loops. The library version vectorizes the
difference function over time windows (already done in the checker) and the
refinement over candidates, with a target of faster than real time at
16 kHz. No compiled code and no new dependency.

The README roadmap (since PR #43) lists F0 tracking as the first item under
"Next, in order": a WORLD-style tracker with a voiced/unvoiced decision,
compared at every time window with cepstral F0. When the tracker lands, that item
moves to Done, pointing to `so.f0_track` and this document, and says plainly
which parts follow Harvest (the refinement) and which do not (the candidate
stage, if D1 is accepted). The References gain YIN, Harvest, DIO and RAPT.

## API sketch

```python
snd = so.load("docs/speech/bdl_arctic_a0131.flac")
trk = so.f0_track(snd)                     # 60-500 Hz, 5 ms hop
trk.t, trk.f0, trk.score, trk.voiced
trk.plot()

# Against cepstral F0, as the gallery could show:
cep = so.Cepstrum(so.STFT(snd, win_dur=0.050, hop_dur=0.005))
t, f0_cep, peak = cep.f0(f_lo=60, f_hi=500)

# Driving the representations that need an F0:
frame = so.TVGaborFrame.pitch_adaptive(trk.t, trk.f0[0], t_end=snd.duration)
```

## Tests (about 4 s added)

- C2: steady vowels at 80–350 Hz within 0.001%; the 1 oct/s glide and the
  vibrato within 0.2% on every time window (0.01% for the steady vowels).
- C3: the score of a steady vowel in white noise matches s / (1 + s) within
  0.03 at 10 and 0 dB SNR (only with the exact shift of D3).
- C5: the high-passed vibrato is tracked with no gross error.
- C6: a second of white noise has no voiced time window.
- C7: on the gallery sentence, at least 99% of time windows voiced by both the
  tracker and the stored Harvest track agree within 5%, and no
  Harvest-unvoiced time window is voiced.
- Multichannel input gives one row per channel; silence gives no voiced
  time window and no warning.
